\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We proposed a nested Markov chain simulator for goal-oriented, human-in-the-loop agent evaluation and outcome attribution. Under a finite set of user personas, absorption and hitting probabilities, trajectory length, sample size, and the publish-as-proxy analysis are closed form, enabling fast experiment design and interpretation. We highlighted four practical takeaways. \textbf{Proxies} that correlate positively with monetization events and occur at higher frequency reduce the sample size needed for A/B tests, enabling faster and cheaper experiments. \textbf{Optimal A/B allocation} is not 50/50: allocating more trajectories to the noisier system maximizes power for a fixed budget, a counterintuitive result that reduces cost and speeds up experiments. \textbf{Calibrating} the chain to data yields leverage: closed-form sensitivities identify which tools and prompt-enhancement points have the highest improvement potential, thereby maximizing return on scarce engineering effort and organizational velocity. \textbf{EM} for transition estimation corrects for label noise, so a perfect labeller is not required. We validated the theory against simulated trajectories and showed that EM recovers transition probabilities under moderate label noise (appendix). Limitations include the focus on discrete personas (continuous and mixture distributions are discussed in \S\ref{sec:method:persona-distributions} and the outlook) and a static chain (dynamics such as post-subscribe behaviour are left for future work).
